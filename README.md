## 📃Overview 
An interactive **Tableau** dashboard designed to evaluate paid acquisition efficiency, unit economics, and 90-day cohort return on investment (ROAS 90d) across channels and geographies. The project transforms raw marketing spend and registration/transaction data into actionable budget insights.

Metrics were calculated in Tableau via Calculated fields.

## 🔧Data preparation

Before visualizing, I joined two tables. To perform the join, I aggregated `users.csv` to the day × channel × geo level (using `registration_date`) and joined it with `expenses.csv`. Used BigQuery and then connected it to Tableau.

<details>
<summary>Joining and aggregating</summary>
  
```
WITH users_agg AS (
  SELECT
    registration_date AS date,
    channel,
    geo,
    COUNT(DISTINCT id_user) AS registrations,
    SUM(is_payer) AS payers,
    ROUND(SUM(revenue_7d), 3) AS revenue_7d,
    ROUND(SUM(revenue_90d), 3) AS revenue_90d
  FROM `Tableau_homework.users`
  GROUP BY date, channel, geo
)
SELECT
  s.date,
  s.channel,
  s.geo,
  s.spend,

  COALESCE(u.registrations, 0) AS registrations,
  COALESCE(u.payers, 0) AS payers,
  COALESCE(u.revenue_7d, 0) AS revenue_7d,
  COALESCE(u.revenue_90d, 0) AS revenue_90d

FROM `Tableau_homework.spend` AS s
LEFT JOIN users_agg AS u
  ON s.date = u.date
  AND s.channel = u.channel
  AND s.geo = u.geo
ORDER BY s.date, s.channel, s.geo;
```
</details>

The results were saved to [`joined_tables.csv`](joined_tables.csv)

## 📊Dashboard architecture

### KPI cards
These specific metrics were chosen because they cover the entire logic: costs → registrations → paying users → revenue → payback.

<img src="/screenshots/KPIs.png" width="900" height="200">

| Metric | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Total Revenue (90 days)** | Total revenue generated by users within 90 days following registration[cite: 3]. Reflects the long-term customer value of acquired cohorts |
| **Total Spend** | Total marketing expenditures across the selected period. Serves as the primary baseline for budget allocation, ROAS, CAC, and Net Profit calculations. |
| **Profit / Loss (90 days)** | Net financial result calculated as `Revenue (90d) - Total Spend`. Highlights whether campaigns generated net profit or operated at a deficit. |
| **ROAS (90 days)** | Return on Ad Spend (`Revenue (90d) / Spend`). The core profitability benchmark measuring how much revenue is generated for every advertising dollar invested. |
| **Registrations** | Total volume of newly registered users. |
| **Payers** | Number of unique users who made at least one purchase. |
| **CAC** | Customer Acquisition Cost. Tracked as **CAC per registration** (cost per lead) and **CAC per payer** (blended cost per acquired paying customer) to balance volume vs. quality. |
| **Payer Conversion** | The percentage of registered users who convert into payers. Evaluates cohort quality and filters high-intent channels from low-quality volume. |
| **ARPU (90 days)** | Average Revenue Per User (`Revenue (90d) / Registrations`). Reflects the average monetization yield across the entire user base. |
| **ARPPU (90 days)** | Average Revenue Per Paying User (`Revenue (90d) / Payers`). Measures spending depth among monetized users across channels and device types. |

### Heatmap ROAS
<img src="/screenshots/Heatmap_ROAS.png" width="400" height="300">

The Heatmap ROAS displays the performance of each channel × geo pair. A standard numerical table would be less visually clear, whereas a heatmap allows you to quickly identify "green" zones with high ROAS (100%+) and "red" zones where costs are not recovered (<100%).

The primary metric chosen for this block is ROAS (90 days), as it directly demonstrates whether a channel recoups invested funds within a specific channel × geo segment. The interpretation baseline is ROAS = 100% / 1.0x: above this threshold, revenue exceeds spend; below it, costs do not break even. This makes the block essential for driving budget reallocation decisions across geographies and channels.

### CAC for payers & ARPPU over time
<img src="/screenshots/CAC_ARPPU.png" width="400" height="300">

This block compares CAC per payer and ARPPU 90d over time. Its goal is to show whether the cost of acquiring a paying user aligns with how much an average paying customer generates.\
It is crucial to analyze these two metrics together. If CAC per payer increases while ARPPU fails to grow alongside it, it signals deteriorating acquisition unit economics. A line chart was chosen because tracking trends and dynamic changes over time is essential here.

### Payer Conversion Rate over time
<img src="/screenshots/Payer_conversion_rate.png" width="400" height="300">

This block displays the dynamic trend of registration-to-payer conversion segmented by geography. Payer Conversion was selected because it reflects the quality of acquired users: high registration volumes on their own do not guarantee business results if users fail to convert into paying customers.

The geographic breakdown is essential because user behavior varies significantly across different markets. It helps assess whether budget reallocation should occur not only across acquisition channels, but also across target countries.

### Channels ROAS over time
<img src="/screenshots/Channels_ROAS.png" width="400" height="400">

The Channels ROAS over time chart illustrates how the return on ad spend for each channel evolves dynamically. It ensures decisions are not based solely on aggregate results over the entire period. A channel might look healthy on average but deteriorate sharply in recent months; conversely, a historically weak channel might show improving momentum.

A line chart was chosen because it is best suited for highlighting trends, seasonality and potential anomalies.

### Revenue vs Spend by channels
<img src="/screenshots/Revenue_Spend.png" width="400" height="500">

This block directly compares 90-day Revenue against Spend across each acquisition channel. It is separated into its own view because ROAS, as a relative ratio, does not always communicate the absolute scale of a problem or an opportunity.

A bar chart is ideal for this comparison, as it immediately highlights channels where expenses heavily exceed incoming revenue, versus channels that deliver substantial revenue relative to their allocated budget.

### Devices performance
<img src="/screenshots/Devices.png" width="500" height="200">

The Devices performance block presents metrics broken down by device OS: Android, iOS, and Web. The selected metrics include registrations, Payer Conversion, ARPU 90d, and ARPPU 90d, helping evaluate whether the operating system impacts paying conversion rates and average monetization value.

Notably, ROAS and CAC are not calculated by device OS in this block because marketing spend data does not include an OS-level breakdown. Consequently, it is impossible to accurately determine how much ad budget was allocated specifically to iOS, Android, or Web.

## 📝Analytical Findings
#### Immature Cohorts (Under 90 Days Lifecycle)


During historical data analysis, a sharp drop in performance metrics was observed in the final months of the timeline. Further investigation revealed this was driven by data characteristics in the `revenue_90d` field.

The dataset contains records in `revenue_90d` even for user registrations where 90 days had not yet elapsed. For instance, records exist for `2026-05-11`, despite the dataset ending on `2026-05-13`, meaning the 90-day post-registration window could not have physically closed. This issue impacts the final months of the dataset: February, March, April, and May 2026.

To validate this hypothesis, the monthly ratio of `revenue_7d / revenue_90d` was analyzed. Across most historical months, it remains stable at approximately 25–30%. However, beginning in March 2026, this ratio spikes sharply: 45.6% in March, 95.3% in April, and 295% in May. This confirms that `revenue_90d` for the closing months is incomplete and immature.

Consequently, these final months severely distort key metrics such as ARPPU, ARPU, and ROAS, directly explaining the steep downward slope on dashboard trendlines. To prevent misleading business interpretations, the dashboard filters exclude these last four months (February, March, April, and May 2026) from the core 90-day revenue analysis.

---
#### Channel-Specific Deep Dive

* **Meta (Q4 2025 Inefficiency):**
  * Spend in Q4 2025 (October–December) grew by ~101% compared to Q3 2025.
  * CAC surged by 71%, while payer conversion remained essentially flat.
  * 90-day ROAS plummeted from ~0.41x down to ~0.24x, and registrations grew by only ~17%.
  * *Diagnosis:* The team rapidly increased spend without proper quality controls or audience scaling thresholds, driving up media costs without a corresponding lift in paying user quality (or indicating potential accounting/tracking discrepancies).

* **TikTok (Structural Underperformance):**
  * Monthly 90-day ROAS hovers persistently between 0.07x and 0.11x.
  * This reflects a systemic, structural issue rather than temporary creative fatigue. The acquired audience either does not match the product's Ideal Customer Profile (ICP) in these regions, or ad messaging creates expectations that the product does not fulfill.

* **Email (High-Performing but Capacity-Constrained):**
  * Profitable across all five target geos, delivering the highest 90-day ROAS: US (3.01x), UK (2.87x), DE (2.55x), PL (2.05x), and UA (1.41x).
  * While highly efficient, it cannot scale infinitely. Incremental budgets should prioritize US, UK, and DE rather than spreading evenly, testing moderate scaling (+50%) rather than aggressive leaps (+300%).

* **Google (Bifurcated Geo Performance):**
  * Performance splits cleanly across markets: profitable in the UK (1.31x) and US (1.29x), but loss-making in DE (0.62x), PL (0.51x), and UA (0.36x).
  * Rather than pausing Google entirely, the primary opportunity lies in intra-channel budget reallocation—trimming underperforming European regions and shifting capital into the UK and US.
